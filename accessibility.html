Accessibility & aging in place

Homes that welcome
everyone who lives in them.

A home should work beautifully for the people inside it — at every stage of life. Dave brings the same craftsmanship to accessibility work that he brings to everything else.

The work

Modifications that
make a real difference.

Dave approaches every accessibility project the same way he approaches fine finish carpentry — with patience, precision, and genuine care for how it looks as well as how it functions.

Dave’s personal mission
“If I ever had the means, I’d spend my time designing homes that people with limited mobility could live in with real dignity — not just safety, but beauty. Until then, I do it one home at a time. It’s some of the most meaningful work I get to do.”
— Dave Pequite

Is this the right time to plan?

Many families find it easier to plan accessibility modifications before they’re urgently needed. Dave is happy to walk through a home and share what he sees — no commitment required.

Talk to Dave about your home
○
Wider doorways

Thoughtful modifications that enhance wheelchair and walker access while honoring the home’s existing design and character. Done cleanly, with care for the finish work around them.

○
Ramps & zero-threshold entries

Beautifully crafted transitions that feel designed and intentional — not retrofitted or institutional. Built to match the home, not stand out from it.

○
Grab bars & safety details

Bathroom and hallway modifications built with real care for aesthetics. As attractive as they are functional — because dignity matters as much as safety.

○
Handrails & stair systems

Solid, precisely built, and designed to give confidence at every step. Crafted to blend with the home, not look like an afterthought.

○
Bathroom modifications

Roll-in shower conversions, bench seating, lowered fixtures — built with dignity and beauty as the guiding principles, not just compliance.

○
Safer entrances & exits

Widened pathways, level thresholds, well-placed support — the modifications that let people stay in their homes and maintain their independence.
